# Model Card: Buyer Credit Risk Score

Follows the spirit of [Mitchell et al., 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.03993). Numbers below are
from `python train.py --report` on the synthetic sample data (`reports/metrics.json`); regenerate
before quoting any number from real data.

## Intended use

- **Task**: for each buyer, as of a given date, estimate the probability that at least one of
  their invoices due in the next 90 days will end up paid more than 60 days late, or unpaid.
- **Intended users**: credit/collections teams at a business that sells on invoiced terms (the
  motivating case here is B2B apparel export/wholesale), to prioritise collections, review credit
  limits, and flag deteriorating buyers earlier than a manual review would.
- **Not intended for**: fully automated credit decisions without human review; consumer credit
  (this is a B2B, invoice-level problem); any buyer with under ~3 invoices of history, where the
  features are mostly missing.

## Training data

Synthetic, generated by `creditrisk/generate.py`: 400 buyers across 5 segments and 10 countries,
~11,600 invoices over roughly 3.5 years, with programmed-in behaviour changes (a share of buyers
gradually deteriorate, and some stop paying entirely) so the label isn't just noise. It is built
to have realistic *shape* -- lateness clustering, disputes correlating with risk, credit-limit
breaches -- not to match any real company's numbers.

**On real data**: replace `data/buyers.csv` and `data/invoices.csv` (schema in the README) and
re-run `train.py`. Expect performance to differ; re-validate before use.

## Label and horizon

- Horizon: 90 days ahead. Severe delinquency: unpaid, or paid more than 60 days late.
- Snapshots are taken every 30 days; a buyer needs roughly 9 months of prior invoice history
  before its features are meaningful, and labels aren't counted until they're fully resolved (see
  `creditrisk/model.py::snapshot_dates`).
- Base rate on the synthetic data: ~7.6% of buyer-snapshots are "bad" in the hold-out.

## Features

~25 features per buyer-snapshot: lateness (average, trend, worst delay, % late/very late),
open and overdue balance and their ratio, credit-limit utilisation, dispute rate, invoice-amount
volatility, tenure, and one-hot segment. Full list and formatting in
`creditrisk/features.py::FEATURE_INFO`. All are computed only from information dated on or before
the snapshot (verified by `tests/test_features.py::test_no_lookahead`).

## Model

Gradient-boosted trees (XGBoost), shallow (`max_depth=3`) and regularised for a small-N,
low-base-rate tabular problem. Hyperparameters and all label/threshold/cost assumptions are in
`creditrisk/config.py`, not hardcoded in the model code.

| Baseline (avg days late, 90d) ROC-AUC / PR-AUC | 0.84 / 0.61 | is the model earning its complexity? |
| Walk-forward, 4 folds | mean 0.895, std 0.010 | is the hold-out result a fluke of one split? |
| Bootstrap 95% CI on hold-out ROC-AUC | [0.85, 0.93] | precision of the point estimate |

All numbers are on buyers/snapshots not used in training, with a gap between train and test equal
to the label horizon so no training label's outcome window overlaps the test period (see
`model.py::holdout_eval`). Full methodology and plots: `notebooks/01_eda_and_model_development.ipynb`
and `reports/`.

### Calibration

Reasonably close to the diagonal on the synthetic data (`reports/calibration.png`) -- a score of
0.5 corresponds to an observed bad rate of about 0.54 in the sharpest bin. Calibration should be
re-checked on real data before treating the score as a literal probability (e.g. for expected-loss
arithmetic) rather than just a ranking.

## Decision thresholds

The dashboard uses fixed score cut-offs (Medium ≥ 0.15, High ≥ 0.40) for interpretability.
`creditrisk/evaluate.py::cost_curve` separately sweeps the threshold against illustrative
unit costs (`config.CostConfig`: cost of missing a default vs. cost of wrongly restricting a good
buyer) and finds a cost-optimal threshold around 0.10 on the synthetic data -- lower than the
current High cut, because the assumed cost of a missed default is much larger than the assumed
cost of over-restricting. **This is a demonstration of the method; the underlying costs are
placeholders and must be replaced with real loss-given-default and margin figures before the
cost-optimal threshold is used operationally.**

## Limitations and risks

- **Synthetic training/eval data.** Every number above describes the pipeline, not real
  creditworthiness. Treat as unvalidated until re-run on real invoices.
- **Label lag.** Outcomes aren't known for up to 150 days after a snapshot (90-day horizon + 60-day
  severity window), so a sudden shift in a buyer's behaviour, or in a whole segment (e.g. a
  macro/FX shock hitting export buyers), is reflected in the training data with a multi-month
  delay.
- **No adverse-action explanation beyond SHAP reason codes.** If this ever gates a live credit
  decision affecting a business's counterparty, the reason codes shown in the app should be
  reviewed by a person before use, and any regulatory disclosure requirements for credit decisions
  in the relevant jurisdictions should be checked independently -- this project does not attempt
  that analysis.
- **Small-N buyers and new buyers.** Buyers with little invoice history fall back on segment-level
  averages via feature imputation; scores for genuinely new buyers should be treated as low
  confidence.
- **No macro or bureau data.** The score only sees a buyer's own invoice history. It cannot see
  the buyer's other creditors, industry stress, or currency risk -- all plausible drivers of
  default for an export business's overseas buyers.
- **Static cost/threshold assumptions.** See "Decision thresholds" above.

## Maintenance

Retrain periodically (e.g. monthly, alongside new invoice data) with `python train.py --report`,
and compare the new `reports/metrics.json` against the previous run before redeploying -- a large
drop in walk-forward AUC or a change in band monotonicity is a signal to investigate before
trusting the new model.
